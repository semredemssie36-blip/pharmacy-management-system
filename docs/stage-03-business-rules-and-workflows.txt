============================================================
PHARMACY ERP SYSTEM
STAGE 03 — BUSINESS RULES AND CORE WORKFLOWS
============================================================

PROJECT:
Multi-Branch Pharmacy Management / ERP System

IMPORTANT:
This stage defines how the pharmacy business operates inside
the system.

This is NOT a coding stage.

DO NOT implement database tables, API endpoints, React pages,
controllers, services, or other application code from this
document alone.

The purpose is to define the business behavior that later
implementation stages must follow.

============================================================
1. PURPOSE
============================================================

The Pharmacy ERP must behave like a real pharmacy business
system rather than a collection of CRUD screens.

Every major business operation must define:

- Actor
- Permission
- Data scope
- Preconditions
- Main workflow
- Business rules
- Exceptions
- Approval requirements
- Inventory effect
- Financial effect
- Audit requirements
- Notification requirements
- Final status

The same business rules must apply regardless of whether the
operation is initiated from the web interface or through an API.

============================================================
2. CORE BUSINESS PRINCIPLE
============================================================

The system must represent REAL BUSINESS EVENTS.

Example:

A purchase order is not inventory.

A receiving transaction creates the inventory effect.

A sale is not merely a form submission.

A completed sale creates a stock movement and financial effect.

A stock adjustment is not direct editing of a quantity.

It is a controlled business event.

A prescription is not the same as a sale.

Dispensing has additional pharmacy and clinical controls.

============================================================
3. GENERAL TRANSACTION LIFECYCLE
============================================================

Major business transactions should generally follow a lifecycle
similar to:

DRAFT
  ↓
SUBMITTED
  ↓
PENDING APPROVAL where required
  ↓
APPROVED
  ↓
EXECUTED / PROCESSED
  ↓
COMPLETED

Possible alternative outcomes:

REJECTED
CANCELLED
VOIDED
PARTIALLY COMPLETED
ON HOLD

Not every transaction requires every status.

The exact statuses must be defined per module.

============================================================
4. MASTER DATA VS TRANSACTION DATA
============================================================

MASTER DATA represents reusable business information.

Examples:

- Product
- Medicine
- Supplier
- Customer
- Patient
- Branch
- Warehouse
- Storage Location
- Category
- Manufacturer
- Pricing configuration

TRANSACTION DATA represents something that happened.

Examples:

- Purchase Order
- Goods Receipt
- Sale
- Prescription
- Dispensing
- Payment
- Return
- Stock Transfer
- Stock Count
- Stock Adjustment
- Disposal

The system must not confuse master data with transactions.

============================================================
5. PRODUCT / MEDICINE BUSINESS RULES
============================================================

A product/medicine may contain:

- Product code
- Barcode
- Brand name
- Generic name
- Active ingredient
- Strength
- Dosage form
- Route
- Category
- Manufacturer
- Registration information
- Prescription classification
- Controlled classification
- Storage requirement
- Minimum stock
- Maximum stock
- Reorder level

Product master data does not represent physical stock.

Example:

Product:
Amoxicillin 500mg

Piassa:
100 boxes

Megenagna:
25 boxes

The product is one master record.

The inventory is branch/warehouse/batch-specific.

============================================================
6. PRODUCT CLASSIFICATION
============================================================

Products may have different operational behavior.

Examples:

- Prescription medicine
- OTC medicine
- Controlled/restricted medicine
- General/FMCG

The classification may affect:

- Sale
- Dispensing
- Authorization
- Prescription requirement
- Audit
- Stock tracking
- Reporting

The system must not treat all products as identical.

8. PURCHASE ORDER RULES
============================================================

A Procurement Officer may create a Purchase Order.

A Purchase Order may contain:

- Supplier
- Branch
- Warehouse
- Products
- Quantities
- Purchase prices
- Currency where applicable
- Expected delivery
- Notes
- Status

A Purchase Order begins as a draft.

Before submission, the creator may edit the draft.

After submission:

- Editing may be restricted.
- Approval may be required.
- Cancellation may follow controlled rules.

IMPORTANT:

Creating a Purchase Order does NOT increase inventory.

Only accepted receiving should create the inventory increase.

============================================================
11. PARTIAL RECEIVING
============================================================

The supplier may deliver less than ordered.

Example:

Ordered:
500 boxes

Delivered:
470 boxes

The system must support partial receiving.

Possible result:

Ordered = 500
Received = 470
Remaining = 30

The Purchase Order must reflect its receiving status.

Possible business outcome:

PARTIALLY RECEIVED

The system must not pretend that 500 were received.

============================================================
14. INVENTORY PRINCIPLE
============================================================

Inventory must be derived from business movements.

Conceptually:

Opening Stock
+ Purchase Receipts
+ Transfers In
+ Sale Returns that become saleable
- Sales
- Dispensing
- Transfers Out
- Damage
- Expiry
- Disposal
± Authorized Adjustments
= Physical Stock

The exact calculation and status model will be designed in the
inventory stage.

============================================================
15. STOCK STATES
============================================================

Physical stock does not automatically mean sellable stock.

Possible stock states include:

- Available
- Reserved
- Quarantined
- Recalled
- Expired
- Damaged
- Other configured blocked states

Example:

Physical stock = 500

Available = 420
Reserved = 30
Quarantined = 20
Recalled = 20
Expired = 10

Total physical quantity = 500

The system must avoid double-counting quantities.

============================================================
16. FEFO RULE
============================================================

FEFO:

First Expire, First Out.

When allocating stock for sale or dispensing, the system should
prefer the earliest-expiring eligible batch.

Example:

Amoxicillin:

Batch A
Expiry: 2027-03-01
Quantity: 10

Batch B
Expiry: 2028-05-31
Quantity: 100

Required:
20

FEFO allocation:

10 from Batch A
10 from Batch B

The system must not randomly choose a later-expiring batch
when an eligible earlier-expiring batch exists, unless an
authorized override is allowed.

============================================================
17. FEFO OVERRIDE
============================================================

An authorized user may need to override FEFO in exceptional
situations.

Example:

Earlier batch is physically inaccessible.

Normal FEFO:
Batch A

Override:
Batch B

The system should:

- Detect the deviation
- Require authorization if configured
- Require reason
- Record the selected batch
- Record the user
- Record the approver if applicable
- Create audit information

The override must never silently bypass FEFO.

============================================================
18. LOW STOCK
============================================================

The system should monitor:

- Minimum stock
- Maximum stock
- Reorder level

Example:

Current stock:
25

Reorder level:
50

The system can identify:

LOW STOCK

But:

LOW STOCK does NOT automatically mean:

"Create purchase order."

The organization may instead:

- Purchase from supplier
- Transfer from another branch
- Use an existing purchase order
- Take another authorized action

The system should support decision-making rather than forcing
one response.

============================================================
48. EXCEPTION VS ERROR VS APPROVAL
============================================================

These concepts must remain distinct.

ERROR:

The operation cannot continue.

Example:

Attempt to dispense expired medicine.

EXCEPTION:

Normal business rule does not fit the current situation.

Example:

FEFO override is needed.

APPROVAL:

An authorized person must decide whether the exception/action
is allowed.

Example:

Manager approves FEFO override.

Conceptually:

ERROR
→ Stop

EXCEPTION
→ Special situation

APPROVAL
→ Authorized decision

49. IMPORTANT ERROR EXAMPLES
============================================================

The system should handle cases such as:

- Insufficient stock
- Expired medicine
- Recalled medicine
- Quarantined stock
- Invalid prescription
- Unauthorized discount
- Credit limit exceeded
- Unauthorized branch access
- Unauthorized warehouse access
- Duplicate barcode
- Invalid batch
- Invalid expiry date
- Receiving quantity greater than allowed
- Transfer discrepancy
- Attempt to modify completed transaction
- Unauthorized approval
- Invalid payment
- Duplicate transaction

============================================================
50. TRANSACTION REVERSAL PRINCIPLE
============================================================

Completed transactions should not normally be silently deleted.

If correction is required, use an appropriate business process.

Examples:

Sale correction:
Return / Void / Reversal

Inventory correction:
Adjustment

Purchase correction:
Supplier return / correction workflow

Payment correction:
Refund / reversal

This preserves business history.

============================================================
51. FINANCIAL EFFECT PRINCIPLE
============================================================

Business transactions may have financial effects.

Examples:

Sale
→ Revenue / receivable / payment

Purchase
→ Purchase value / payable

Return
→ Financial reversal

Discount
→ Reduces sale value

Customer payment
→ Reduces receivable

Supplier payment
→ Reduces payable

The system must keep financial records connected to the
transaction that caused them.

============================================================
52. REPORTING PRINCIPLE
============================================================

Reports must be generated from actual business records.

Example:

Sales report should come from completed sales.

Inventory report should come from inventory data/movements.

Purchase report should come from procurement records.

Profitability should use actual transaction/cost information
according to the configured costing model.

Reports must respect user permissions and data scope.

============================================================
62. BUSINESS RULE PRIORITY
============================================================

When implementing features, prioritize:

1. Correct inventory behavior
2. Correct permissions
3. Correct transaction states
4. Correct approval behavior
5. Correct financial effects
6. Correct audit trail
7. Correct reporting
8. UI convenience

A beautiful UI must never override a business rule.

============================================================
63. AI IMPLEMENTATION RULE
============================================================

AI coding agents must NOT invent business behavior.

Before implementing a module, the agent must understand:

- Actors
- Permissions
- Scope
- Workflow
- Business rules
- Exceptions
- Inventory effects
- Financial effects
- Approval requirements
- Audit requirements

If something is not defined:

DO NOT silently invent a major business rule.

Mark it as:

UNDEFINED / REQUIRES DESIGN DECISION

and continue only when a safe implementation is possible.
