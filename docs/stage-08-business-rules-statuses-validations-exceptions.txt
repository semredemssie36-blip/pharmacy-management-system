============================================================
STAGE 08 — BUSINESS RULES, STATUSES, VALIDATIONS & EXCEPTIONS
============================================================

Project:
Pharmacy Management System

Purpose:
This document defines how important business transactions must behave.

IMPORTANT:
Stage 07 defined the detailed modules.
Stage 08 defines the rules that control those modules.

The system must not be implemented as a collection of simple CRUD screens.

Every important business transaction must have:

- Preconditions
- Required inputs
- Validation rules
- Status
- Allowed status transitions
- Permission requirements
- Data-scope requirements
- Approval/override requirements where applicable
- Inventory effect
- Financial effect
- Audit effect
- Notification effect where applicable
- Exception/error handling
- Transaction integrity
- Rollback behavior where applicable

The backend must enforce business rules.

The frontend may guide users and hide unavailable actions, but frontend
controls are NOT the security or business-rule enforcement layer.


============================================================
1. GENERAL TRANSACTION LIFECYCLE
============================================================

A general business transaction may follow:

Create
  ↓

  ↓
Submit
  ↓

  ↓
Execute
  ↓

  ↓

  ↓

  ↓

Not every transaction requires every stage.

Example:

Simple POS sale:

  ↓

  ↓
Complete

Stock adjustment:

  ↓
Submit
  ↓
Pending Approval
  ↓
Approve
  ↓
Apply Adjustment
  ↓
Inventory Update
  ↓
Audit


============================================================
2. IMPORTANT RULE ABOUT APPROVALS
============================================================

Approval requirements must be configurable.

Do NOT hardcode statements such as:

"Only Branch Manager can approve every transaction."

Instead, the system must support configurable approval rules.

The approval engine must be capable of determining:

- Which transaction requires approval
- Under what condition approval is required
- Who is allowed to approve
- Whether one or multiple approvals are required
- Whether approval is mandatory or optional
- What happens after approval
- What happens after rejection

The source requirements explicitly identify configurable approval/override
requirements for:

- Stock adjustments
- Price changes
- Discounts
- Credit-limit overrides
- Expired/near-expiry overrides
- FEFO overrides
- Prescription exceptions
- Clinical warning overrides
- Controlled-medicine exceptions
- Returns
- Purchase orders
- Stock disposal

------------------------------------------------------------

- Products exist and are active where required
- Quantity is greater than zero
- Valid purchasing unit is used
- Agreed price is valid
- Required destination exists
- User has permission to create the PO

Creating or approving a Purchase Order must NOT automatically increase
inventory.

Example:

25

Inventory = 25

Inventory increases only when goods are actually received.

============================================================
9. FEFO RULES
============================================================

FEFO means:

First Expiry, First Out.


The system must:

- Identify available batches
- Prioritize earliest expiry
- Prevent expired stock from being selected
- Warn/block near-expiry stock according to configured policy
- Reserve selected stock during dispensing
- Record FEFO allocation
- Audit FEFO overrides


Example:

Batch A:
Expiry = 2027
Quantity = 10

Batch B:
Expiry = 2028
Quantity = 100

Required quantity:

20

Normal FEFO allocation:

Batch A = 10
Batch B = 10


The system must not simply select any batch randomly.


------------------------------------------------------------
9.1 FEFO Override
------------------------------------------------------------

If the user wants to use a later-expiring batch instead of the FEFO
recommended batch:

The system must:

1. Detect FEFO deviation
2. Warn the user
3. Request reason
4. Require approval if configured
5. Record the override
6. Record who performed it
7. Record date/time
8. Preserve audit history

============================================================
19. STOCK STATUS MODEL
============================================================

The system must support stock statuses including:

- Available
- Reserved
- Quarantined
- Damaged
- Expired
- Recalled
- Returned
- Awaiting Disposal
- Disposed


These statuses represent different business conditions.

They must not all be treated as sellable stock.


------------------------------------------------------------
19.1 Available Stock
------------------------------------------------------------

Available stock can normally be considered for sale/dispensing if all
other business rules are satisfied.


------------------------------------------------------------
19.2 Reserved Stock
------------------------------------------------------------

Reserved stock is associated with an active transaction and should not
be freely allocated to another transaction.


------------------------------------------------------------
19.3 Quarantined Stock
------------------------------------------------------------

Quarantined stock physically exists but must not be treated as normally
available stock.


------------------------------------------------------------
19.4 Damaged Stock
------------------------------------------------------------

Damaged stock must not be treated as normal available stock unless an
authorized business process changes its status.


------------------------------------------------------------
19.5 Expired Stock
------------------------------------------------------------

Expired stock cannot be sold or dispensed.


------------------------------------------------------------
19.6 Recalled Stock
------------------------------------------------------------

Recalled stock must be restricted from normal sale/dispensing according
to the recall workflow.


------------------------------------------------------------
19.7 Returned Stock
------------------------------------------------------------

Returned stock requires evaluation before becoming available again.


------------------------------------------------------------
19.8 Awaiting Disposal
------------------------------------------------------------

Stock awaiting disposal must not be available for normal transactions.


------------------------------------------------------------
19.9 Disposed
------------------------------------------------------------

Disposed stock is no longer available as inventory.

============================================================
29. ERROR VS WARNING VS EXCEPTION
============================================================

The system must distinguish these concepts.


------------------------------------------------------------
29.1 ERROR
------------------------------------------------------------

An error means the transaction cannot continue.

Example:

Expired medicine selected.

Result:

Sale blocked.


Another example:

Prescription quantity = 20

User attempts to dispense:

25

Result:

Dispensing blocked.


------------------------------------------------------------
29.2 WARNING
------------------------------------------------------------

A warning informs the user of a potential problem.

Example:

Medicine is near expiry.


The system may:

- Allow continuation
- Require confirmation
- Require approval

depending on configured policy.


------------------------------------------------------------
29.3 EXCEPTION / OVERRIDE
------------------------------------------------------------

An exception occurs when the normal rule does not fit the situation and
an authorized decision may be required.


Example:

FEFO selects Batch A.

User wants Batch B.

This is a FEFO override.


Another:

Customer exceeds credit limit.

Authorized user may request credit override.

============================================================
33. ATOMIC TRANSACTION RULE
============================================================

Critical business operations must be handled atomically.

Example:

Completing a sale may involve:

1. Create sale
2. Create sale items
3. Validate inventory
4. Update inventory
5. Create stock movement
6. Create payment
7. Update customer balance if applicable
8. Create audit record


The system must not produce a false successful result such as:

Sale created = YES
Payment = YES
Inventory update = NO
Stock movement = NO


while telling the user:

"Sale completed."


Critical operations must either:

- Complete all required business effects successfully

or:

- Roll back safely


Conceptually:

BEGIN TRANSACTION

Create Sale
Create Sale Items
Validate Stock
Update Inventory
Create Stock Movement
Create Payment
Update Customer Balance
Create Audit

COMMIT


If a critical step fails:

ROLLBACK


The implementation must use the backend/database transaction mechanism
appropriate for the selected architecture.

============================================================
36. GLOBAL VALIDATION PRINCIPLES
============================================================

Validation must occur at multiple levels.


------------------------------------------------------------
36.1 Required Field Validation
------------------------------------------------------------

Examples:

Product name required.

Supplier required.

Quantity must be greater than zero.

Batch number may be required depending on transaction/product rules.

Expiry date may be required depending on the product/transaction.


------------------------------------------------------------
36.2 Business Validation
------------------------------------------------------------

Examples:

Cannot dispense expired medicine.

Cannot sell unavailable stock.

Cannot dispense beyond prescription quantity.

Cannot perform unauthorized refill.

Cannot bypass required prescription.

Cannot approve a transaction without approval permission.

Cannot use an inaccessible branch/warehouse.


------------------------------------------------------------
36.3 Permission Validation
------------------------------------------------------------

Before sensitive operations:

Does the current user have permission?

Example:

Inventory
→ Stock Adjustment
→ Approve


The system must verify the user's permission.


------------------------------------------------------------
36.4 Data Scope Validation
------------------------------------------------------------

Permission alone is not enough.

The system must also verify:

Does the user have access to this branch?

Does the user have access to this warehouse?

Does the user have access to this organization?


Example:

Piassa manager should not automatically see all Megenagna operational
data merely because the user has "Branch Manager" role.


------------------------------------------------------------
36.5 Approval Validation
------------------------------------------------------------

If approval is required:

Has approval been granted?

If not:

Do not execute the sensitive operation.


------------------------------------------------------------
36.6 Data Integrity Validation
------------------------------------------------------------

The system must verify:

- Referenced records exist
- Referenced records are valid
- Product belongs to transaction
- Batch exists
- Batch belongs to product
- Warehouse exists
- Location belongs to warehouse
- Source and destination are valid
- Inventory quantity is sufficient where required
- Prescription belongs to patient where applicable


============================================================
37. STATUS TRANSITION RULE
============================================================

Statuses must not be freely editable.


For example, a user must not be able to manually change:

Draft

directly to:

Completed

unless the business process allows that transition.


Every important status must have:

- Allowed previous statuses
- Allowed next statuses
- Required permissions
- Required validations
- Required approvals
- Business effects


Example:

Purchase Order:

Draft
→ Submitted

Allowed.

Purchase Order:

Draft
→ Fully Received

Not allowed.

============================================================
40. EXCEPTION HANDLING PRINCIPLE
============================================================

Every major module must define what happens when the normal process fails.


Examples:


Scenario:
Insufficient stock

Result:
Sale/dispensing cannot continue unless the business workflow supports
an alternative such as partial dispensing.


Scenario:
Expired stock selected

Result:
Block transaction.


Scenario:
Prescription expired

Result:
Block dispensing unless a configured authorized exception workflow
exists.


Scenario:
Credit limit exceeded

Result:
Warn/block/approval according to configuration.


Scenario:
FEFO override requested

Result:
Warn + reason + approval if configured.


Scenario:
Goods received quantity differs from PO

Result:
Record discrepancy.


Scenario:
Returned medicine is unsuitable

Result:
Quarantine.


Scenario:
Stock count differs from system quantity

Result:
Variance review + adjustment process.


Scenario:
User lacks branch scope

Result:
Access denied.


Scenario:
Approval rejected

Result:
Sensitive action must not be applied.

============================================================

When multiple rules apply, the system must evaluate them in a safe order.


Recommended conceptual order:

1. Authentication
2. Permission
3. Data scope
4. Record existence
5. Record status
6. Required fields

The final implementation may organize services differently as long as the

============================================================
42. IMPORTANT BUSINESS DISTINCTIONS
============================================================

The system must preserve these distinctions:


Customer ≠ Patient

Patient:
Clinical/pharmacy context.

Customer:
Commercial/customer account context.


Purchase Order ≠ Goods Receipt

PO:
What the pharmacy ordered.

Goods Receipt:
What the pharmacy physically received.


Goods Receipt ≠ Inventory Movement

Goods Receipt:
Business receiving transaction.

Inventory Movement:
Actual stock ledger effect.


Sale ≠ Payment

Sale:
Commercial transaction.

Payment:
Settlement of financial obligation.


Prescription ≠ Dispensing

Prescription:
Authorization/instruction for medicine.

Dispensing:
Actual medicine issuance.


Return ≠ Automatic Restocking

Returned medicine must be evaluated first.


Quarantine ≠ Disposal

Quarantine:
Stock is restricted.

Disposal:
Stock is permanently removed according to the disposal workflow.


Recall ≠ Expiry

Recall:
Product/batch is affected by a recall event.

Expiry:
Product has reached its expiry condition.


Warning ≠ Error

Warning:
Potential problem.

Error:
Transaction cannot continue.


Exception ≠ Normal Workflow

Exception:
Requires special handling or authorization.


Role ≠ Data Scope

Role:
What actions the user may perform.

Scope:
Which organization/branch/warehouse data the user may access.


Physical Stock ≠ Available Stock

Physical stock may include:

- Available
- Reserved
- Quarantined
- Damaged
- Expired
- Recalled
- Returned
- Awaiting disposal
